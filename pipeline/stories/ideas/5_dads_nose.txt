Me|My dad walked into my parent-teacher conference, and his nose was so long it knocked the principal's coffee onto the floor.
Me|That was my fault. I had just said I did my homework.
Me|You see, a month earlier, I found out about the rule. Every time I lie, my dad's nose grows.
Me|Not mine. His.
Me|I told my mom I brushed my teeth. In the kitchen, Dad's nose hit the fridge.
Dad|Who lied?
Me|I said, "Not me." His nose went through the window.
Me|So I tried to stop lying. That lasted about four minutes.
Me|Grandma asked if I liked her new sweater. Dad's nose knocked a bird out of a tree.
Me|The neighbors started hanging their coats on it. The mailman used it as a ramp.
Me|At work, they made him sit by the door. He held it open with his nose.
Me|Then I noticed something weird. Sometimes his nose grew, and I hadn't said anything.
Me|At dinner, Mom asked him how work was.
Dad|Great. Really great.
Me|Boing. His nose hit the lamp. I hadn't said a word.
Me|The next morning, he left the house in his tie at eight a.m.
Dad|Off to the office!
Me|Boing.
Me|So I followed him. He didn't go to the office. He went to the park and sat on a bench all day. In his tie.
Me|He had lost his job a month ago. He didn't want us to worry.
Me|That's when I understood. The rule wasn't about my lies. It was about every lie in our house.
Me|That night at dinner, Mom asked him again.
Mom|How was work today?
Me|Dad looked at me. I looked at Dad. His nose was already shaking.
Me|Then he put his fork down.
Dad|I don't have a job anymore. I'm sorry.
Me|His nose went pop and shrank back to normal.
Me|Mom hugged him. I hugged him. Grandma admitted her sweater was ugly anyway.
Me|Nobody's nose grew. Not even a little.
Me|Now our family has one rule. No lies. Not even small ones.
Me|Dad found a new job. They hired him because he's honest. Very honest.
Dad|Son, your haircut looks like a broom.
Me|Which brings us back to the parent-teacher conference.
Me|The teacher looked at Dad's nose. Then at me.
Me|I said, "I didn't do my homework." His nose shrank right back.
Me|I got detention. Dad got a new coffee for the principal. Worth it.
