Me|My eighty-year-old grandpa sat down next to me in math class. He was ten.
Teacher|New student? What's your name?
Grandpa|Harold. I have a pension.
Me|You see, two weeks earlier, my mom dropped me off at Grandpa's house for the summer.
Me|No Wi-Fi. No video games. Just a cat named Doug and a clock that ticked really loud.
Me|On day one, I said, "I'm so bored." Grandpa's back cracked. Then it uncracked.
Me|He stood up straight for the first time in fifteen years. His gray hair turned brown. He was seventy.
Me|The next day, I said it again. He was sixty. He did a cartwheel. He hadn't done one since 1974.
Me|That's when I figured out the rule. Every time I say "I'm bored," Grandpa gets ten years younger.
Me|At first, it was awesome. At forty, he beat me at basketball. At thirty, he bought a motorcycle.
Me|Then I said it twice in one afternoon. He was ten.
Mom|Why is a child answering Grandpa's phone?
Me|So I made a plan. Never say "I'm bored" again.
Me|Attempt one. I said, "I'm unentertained." He lost five years. Close enough counts.
Me|Attempt two. I yawned. His front teeth fell out. Baby teeth.
Me|Attempt three. I stayed super busy. I cleaned the garage, mowed the lawn, and alphabetized his spices.
Me|Then I sighed. Just once. He started teething.
Me|Grandpa was four years old. He wanted juice boxes and naps. He bit the mailman.
Me|That night, I found his old birthday card on the fridge. Under "my wish," he had written something.
Me|"I wish I wasn't too old for my grandson."
Me|That's when it hit me. Every time I said I was bored at his house, he thought I meant him.
Me|I went to his room. He was coloring. Inside the lines. Very carefully.
Me|I said, "Grandpa, I don't need you young. I need you to teach me the stuff you know."
Grandpa|Like what?
Me|Like how to fish. How to fix stuff. That card game where everybody yells.
Me|He looked at me for a long time. Then he hopped off the bed.
Grandpa|It's called Spit. And you're gonna lose.
Me|We played until midnight. I lost forty times. I laughed every single time.
Me|And every time I laughed, he got older. Ten years. Twenty. Fifty. All the way back to eighty.
Me|His creaky back. His loud clock. His cat named Doug.
Me|Best summer of my life. I didn't say I was bored once.
Me|Which brings us back to math class.
Me|It was a really boring class. I said it seven times.
Me|Now Grandpa is ten again. And he's better at math than me.
Teacher|Harold, please stop correcting the textbook.
