Me|My dad was reading the weather on live TV when his nose shot through the screen and knocked over a camera.
Me|That was my fault. I had just told my mom I cleaned my room.
Me|You see, two weeks earlier, I found out about the rule. Every time I lie, my dad's nose grows.
Me|Not mine. His.
Me|I told my teacher my dog ate my homework. Across town, Dad's nose popped a balloon at a kid's birthday party.
Me|I told Grandma her fruitcake was delicious. Dad's nose rang a church bell.
Dad|Who is lying?!
Me|I said, "Not me." Boing.
Me|So I made a plan. I would stop lying. Completely.
Me|Attempt one. I stopped talking. Mom asked if I was okay. I nodded. Dad's nose grew anyway.
Me|Turns out, nodding counts.
Me|Attempt two. I taped my mouth shut. A kid asked if I liked his drawing. I gave him a thumbs up.
Me|Dad's nose knocked a helicopter out of the sky. A toy helicopter. Still.
Me|Attempt three. Only the truth. All the time.
Teacher|Do you like my new haircut?
Me|I said it looked like a mop. I told the principal he smells like soup.
Me|I got detention. The principal started wearing cologne. It smells like soup.
Me|But Dad's nose kept growing. Even when I didn't say anything.
Me|It grew every night at seven. Exactly when Dad called to say goodnight from the TV station.
Dad|Sorry, buddy. Busy night. I'll make it up to you.
Me|I said, "It's fine. I don't care."
Me|Boing.
Me|That's when I figured it out. "I don't care" was the biggest lie I ever told.
Me|Dad had missed my last six games, my birthday, and the science fair. Every time, I said it was fine.
Me|It wasn't fine.
Me|So the next morning, I went to the TV station. Dad was about to go live. His nose was already three feet long.
Producer|Ten seconds! Somebody shorten his nose!
Me|I walked right onto the set.
Me|Dad, I do care. I miss you. I want you at my game.
Me|His nose went pop and shrank back to normal. On live TV. In front of two million people.
Me|Dad looked at the camera. Then at me. Then he took off his microphone.
Dad|Today's forecast: a hundred percent chance I'm going to my son's game.
Me|He walked off the set. The producer fainted.
Me|He made it to the game. He cheered so loud the referee gave him a yellow card.
Me|We lost by twenty points. Best game of my life.
Me|Now I don't lie anymore. Not even small ones.
Me|Which brings us back to the weather.
Me|Last night, Dad said tomorrow would be sunny. It snowed two feet.
Me|His nose didn't grow. He's just bad at weather.
