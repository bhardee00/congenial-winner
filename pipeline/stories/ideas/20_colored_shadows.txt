IN THIS WORLD, EVERY SHADOW HAS A COLOR (idea A, mirrors the reference #5 script line by line) - narrator only

1. In this world, every shadow has a color. Pink. Blue. Gold.
2. You see, when the sun hits you, your shadow shows who you are.
3. My mom's shadow is rose pink. My dad's is ocean blue. The mailman's is sunflower yellow.
4. You always know who's coming down the street, just by the color on the sidewalk.
5. And when the whole town stands together at sunset, it makes one giant rainbow.
6. Once a year, at the Sunset Festival, everyone stands in the square at the same time, and the rainbow stretches over the whole valley.
7. Except me.
8. When I stand in the sun, nothing. No color. No shadow. Not even gray.
9. Doctors couldn't fix it. Kids called me Ghost Kid.
10. And my parents never let me go to the festival.
11. "We don't want you to leave a hole in the rainbow, sweetie."
12. So every year, I watched from my bedroom window.
13. But here's the weird thing. Every year, the rainbow got worse.
14. The colors started bleeding together. The blues got too dark. The reds got too bright.
15. People stood closer and closer trying to fix it, and the rainbow just got muddier.
16. Flowers wilted. Birds flew away.
17. Last year, the shadows got so dark, they cracked the lamp on the clock tower.
18. Nobody knew why it was happening.
19. But I noticed something. Whenever I walked into a room, everyone's shadows got lighter. Softer. Clearer. Like they could finally breathe.
20. The day before the festival, I snuck into the old library, and on the wall, in a glass case, was the very first painting of the town rainbow.
21. Painted two hundred years ago. Every color, for every family in town.
22. And right in the middle, there was a spot I'd never seen before. A little white gap.
23. Under it, someone had written one word. Light.
24. Light is the space between colors. The clear part. The part that lets every color shine.
25. And next to it was a date. My birthday. Twelve years ago.
26. The rainbow had been getting worse for twelve years. Ever since I was born. Ever since my parents started keeping me home.
27. The town wasn't broken. It was missing its light. It was missing me.
28. Festival day, the whole town crowded into the square. The colors smeared together. Brown. Gray. Mud.
29. It was awful. People covered their eyes. The clock tower went dark.
30. And I walked right into the middle of the square.
31. Everyone stopped.
32. "Ghost Kid, get out of the festival!"
33. But I didn't move. I just stood there, right in the center.
34. The whole town had to step around me. And as every single person stepped around me, their shadow went clear for one second.
35. And then the colors came back, but different.
36. The blues softened. The reds found their place. Every color fit together.
37. For the first time in twelve years, the town rainbow was beautiful.
38. It stretched over the whole valley. Butterflies started following it.
39. And the cracked lamp on the clock tower lit up, brighter than ever.
40. My mom pushed through the crowd. She took my hands. "We thought you were broken," she whispered.
41. "Mom," I said, "the colors were just waiting for some light."
42. Now every year, I stand in the middle of the festival. I still don't have a shadow. And the rainbow has never been brighter.
