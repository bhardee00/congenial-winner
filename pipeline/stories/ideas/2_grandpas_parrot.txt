Me|I was standing at my grandpa's funeral when his parrot flew onto the coffin and yelled my bank password to two hundred people.
Parrot|Password one two three!
Me|That's not my... okay, that's my password.
Me|You see, a week earlier, my grandpa's will said I'd get one million dollars if I could keep his parrot quiet for one full day.
Lawyer|Twenty-four hours. Not one word.
Me|That's it?
Lawyer|That's it.
Me|I signed immediately. How hard could it be to keep a bird quiet?
Me|Well, the parrot's name was Captain. And Captain had been listening to everything in that house for forty years.
Me|He knew every secret, every swear word, and the Wi-Fi password.
Me|Hour one, I gave him a cracker.
Parrot|Thank you, loser!
Me|He said that to me! That doesn't count!
Lawyer|It counts double.
Lawyer|Starting over.
Me|So I covered his cage with a blanket. That's how you put birds to sleep.
Me|He fell asleep, all right. Then he started sleep talking. In Grandpa's voice.
Parrot|Don't tell my grandson about the money in the freezer.
Me|I froze. Then I ran to the freezer.
Me|Inside was one frozen chicken nugget and a note that said, "Nice try."
Me|I'm not proud of it. I ate the nugget.
Lawyer|Starting over.
Me|Hour six, I taught him sign language so he wouldn't need to talk.
Me|He learned it in ten minutes. Then he signed something I can't repeat.
Me|By hour twelve, I was desperate. I put headphones on him and played whale sounds.
Me|He loved it. He started singing along. Like, professionally. My neighbor started filming.
Neighbor|Bro, this bird can sing!
Me|The video got a million views. The lawyer said singing counts as words.
Lawyer|Starting over. Also, can I get an autograph?
Me|Hour eighteen, I tried to bore him to sleep. I read him Grandpa's taxes.
Me|He fell asleep in two minutes. Then he woke up and corrected my math.
Parrot|Wrong!
Lawyer|Starting over.
Me|So I made a plan. Final attempt. I'd sit in front of him in total silence. No TV. No phone. No crackers.
Me|Hour twenty-three. Fifty-nine minutes. I was staring at him. He was staring at me.
Me|Then the doorbell rang. It was my cousin Derek. Grandpa's other grandson.
Derek|Hey, Captain! Say "Derek is the best"!
Derek|Come on, buddy. Who's the favorite grandson? Say Derek!
Me|Derek wanted the money too. He even brought crackers. The good ones.
Me|Captain opened his beak. I couldn't breathe.
Me|Then he closed it, flew across the room, and landed on my shoulder.
Me|The clock hit twenty-four hours. Silence.
Lawyer|Congratulations. The million is yours.
Me|Derek dropped to his knees. I couldn't believe it.
Me|Why did you stay quiet, Captain?
Me|He leaned in and whispered in Grandpa's voice.
Parrot|Because you stayed.
Me|I wanted to cry. Every Sunday for ten years, I visited Grandpa. Derek visited once. For the Wi-Fi.
Me|Grandpa always told me, "Anybody can talk. Not everybody listens."
Me|Captain wasn't the test. The visits were.
Me|Then the lawyer cleared his throat.
Lawyer|One more thing. You also inherit Captain.
Me|Which brings us back to the funeral.
Parrot|Password one two three!
Me|I changed my password. He already knows the new one.
