Me|I was trapped in a bouncy castle with a billionaire's kid screaming "best friends forever" while a mariachi band played our friendship song.
Me|We had known each other for six days.
Me|You see, a week earlier, a rich kid named Tucker sat down next to me at lunch with a briefcase.
Tucker|Twenty thousand dollars. Be my best friend for one week.
Me|That's it?
Tucker|That's it. And you can't tell anyone I paid you.
Me|I shook his hand immediately. How hard could being a best friend be?
Me|Well, day one, he made us matching jackets. They had our faces on them. Each other's faces.
Me|Day two, he hired a photographer to follow us everywhere. To "capture the memories."
Tucker|Smile like you mean it. That costs extra.
Me|Day three, I tried to sit with my real friends at lunch. Tucker bought the table.
Me|Like, the actual table. He had it delivered to his house.
Me|So I decided to get fired. I would be the worst best friend ever.
Me|I forgot his birthday. He moved his birthday.
Me|I beat him at every video game. He clapped. Then he framed my controller.
Me|I told him his jokes weren't funny. He hired a comedy writer. The comedy writer wasn't funny either.
Tucker|Two more days. Then the money is yours.
Me|Day six, he threw us a friendship party. Bouncy castle. Mariachi band. A cake shaped like my face.
Me|Which brings us back to the bouncy castle.
Me|Nobody came. Not one kid from school. Just me, Tucker, the band, and his butler.
Me|I asked the butler where everyone else was.
Butler|Sir, you are the first friend to ever show up.
Me|Then I found Tucker's notebook. Every page was a list called "Friends I could buy."
Me|Every name was crossed out. Except mine. Mine had a little heart next to it.
Me|I felt terrible. I was the only friend he had, and he was paying me to be there.
Me|On day seven, he handed me the briefcase.
Tucker|Here. Twenty thousand. You can go now.
Me|I opened it. Then I closed it.
Me|What if I stay for free?
Tucker|Friends don't work for free.
Me|Yeah, they do. That's the whole point.
Me|He stared at me like I'd spoken another language.
Tucker|So... you'd still sit with me at lunch?
Me|Only if you give the table back.
Me|He gave the table back. The next week, he said hi to three kids. Without a briefcase.
Me|Now he has eleven friends. He didn't pay any of them.
Me|I'm still number one. It says so on my jacket.
Me|Then the mariachi band showed up at my house.
Bandleader|He paid us for a whole year.
