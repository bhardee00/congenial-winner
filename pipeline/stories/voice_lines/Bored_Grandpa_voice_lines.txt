========== FILE 1: NARRATOR (speed 1.60x) ==========

My eighty-year-old grandpa sat down next to me in math class. He was ten.

You see, two weeks earlier, my mom dropped me off at Grandpa's house for the whole summer.

No Wi-Fi. No video games. Just a cat named Doug and a clock that ticked really loud.

On day one, I said, "I'm so bored." Grandpa's back cracked. Then it uncracked.

He stood up straight for the first time in fifteen years. His gray hair turned brown. He was seventy.

The next day, I said it again. He was sixty. He did a cartwheel. He hadn't done one since 1974.

That's when I figured out the rule. Every time I say "I'm bored," Grandpa gets ten years younger.

At first, it was awesome.

At fifty, he signed up for a marathon. He won. He was the only one there.

At forty, he beat me at basketball. Then he dunked on the mailman.

At thirty, he bought a motorcycle. And a leather jacket. And sunglasses he wore inside.

At twenty, he asked for the Wi-Fi password. There was no Wi-Fi. So he installed Wi-Fi.

Then I said it twice in one afternoon. He was ten.

I said, "Wrong number," and hung up. That's when I knew I had a problem.

So I made a plan. Never say "I'm bored" again.

Attempt one. I said, "I'm unentertained." He lost five years. Close enough counts.

Attempt two. I made a bored jar. Every time I almost said it, I put in a dollar. I went broke by lunch.

Attempt three. I yawned. His front teeth fell out. Baby teeth.

Attempt four. I stayed super busy. I cleaned the garage, mowed the lawn, and alphabetized his spices.

Then I sighed. Just once. He started teething.

Grandpa was four years old.

He wanted naps. He wanted cartoons. He bit the mailman.

I had to read him a bedtime story. He fell asleep on page one. Then he woke up and corrected my reading.

That night, I found his old birthday card on the fridge. Under "my wish," he had written something.

"I wish I wasn't too old for my grandson."

That's when it hit me. Every time I said I was bored at his house, he thought I meant him.

He thought he was the boring part. He thought he had to change.

I went to his room. He was coloring. Inside the lines. Very carefully.

I said, "Grandpa, I don't need you young. I need you to teach me the stuff you know."

Like how to fish. How to fix stuff. That card game where everybody yells.

He looked at me for a long time. Then he hopped off the bed.

We played until midnight. I lost forty times. I laughed every single time.

And every time I laughed, he got older. Ten years. Twenty. Fifty. All the way back to eighty.

His creaky back. His loud clock. His cat named Doug.

The next morning, he taught me to fish. I caught a boot. He caught the same boot. We argued about it for an hour.

Then he showed me his rock collection. It's still mostly gravel. I loved it anyway.

Best summer of my life. I didn't say I was bored once.

Which brings us back to math class.

It was a really boring class. I said it seven times.

Now Grandpa is ten again. He sits next to me. He's better at math than me.
