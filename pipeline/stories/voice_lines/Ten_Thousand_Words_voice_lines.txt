TEN THOUSAND WORDS - voice lines (ElevenLabs Adam, change audio pitch ON)

========== FILE 1: NARRATOR (speed 1.60x) ==========

In my town, everyone is born with ten thousand words. When your counter hits zero, your voice is gone. Forever.

The number glows right on your chest, so everyone knows exactly how much you've got left.

So nobody here talks much.

Mr. Henderson orders at the diner by pointing. Pancakes. Syrup. The waitress. She's his wife.

The mayor gives his speeches on cue cards. Forty cards. The last one just says "Thanks."

Grandma Pearl has nine thousand words saved. She says she's saving them for something important.

She's been saying that for thirty years.

Then there's Mr. Gibbs. His counter says zero. He used his last word at a football game. The word was "fumble." It wasn't even a fumble.

And then there's me.

I narrated every video game I played. Out loud.

I gave my dog Biscuit live commentary every time he chased a squirrel.

I sang in the shower. Every song. Both parts.

I thought ten thousand was basically infinity.

It wasn't.

On my eleventh birthday, I blew out the candles and looked down at my chest.

Fourteen.

That cost her eight words. She didn't care.

After that, I stopped talking. No "good morning." No "thanks." Not even "bless you."

At dinner, my little brother Max asked me for the ketchup.

I just stared at him. I wanted to answer so bad.

So I went to the library and got a book on sign language. I practiced every night in the mirror.

Hello. Thank you. Help. And Max's favorite sign: freeze.

Because Max and I had a game. When I made the sign for freeze, he had to stop and not move a muscle. Max was undefeated.

Then came the block party.

The whole town was there. Nobody was talking, of course. Just chewing.

Max chased a balloon right into the street.

Behind him, the ice cream truck started backing up. Beep. Beep. Beep.

The driver couldn't see him. Max couldn't hear the beeping over the music.

Fifty people saw it. Fifty people had words to spare. Nobody said a thing.

Mr. Henderson pointed. Grandma Pearl opened her mouth. Then she closed it.

So I ran. And I yelled.

Ten words. He didn't even turn around. The music was too loud.

I had four left.

On the last word, my voice just stopped. Like someone unplugged it. Zero.

But Max turned around. He saw me.

So I did the only thing I had left. I made the sign for freeze.

Max froze. Mid-step. Not one muscle.

The truck rolled past him. Six inches away.

The driver finally saw him and slammed the brakes. Ice cream went everywhere.

Max ran over and hugged me. Then he signed something back. He'd been practicing too. Thank you.

My mom squeezed us so hard I would've said "ow," if I could.

Then Grandma Pearl walked into the middle of the street.

Turns out, that was the something important.

After that, the town changed.

Everyone learned sign language. The mayor signs his speeches now. They're way shorter.

Mr. Gibbs signs "fumble" at every game. It's still never a fumble.

And me? I've got zero words left.

But I've never been heard more.
